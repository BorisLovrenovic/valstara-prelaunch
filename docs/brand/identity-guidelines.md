## 1. Brand foundation
 
### 1.1 Name — LOCKED
 
**Valstara** · valstara.com
 
Written as one word, capital V, remaining letters lowercase. Not "ValStara," not "VALSTARA" in body copy or interface.
 
Legal entity: **3NITY Digital Limited**, Hong Kong. The entity name appears only where legally required — terms, invoices, footer. Valstara is the brand; 3NITY is the company.
 
### 1.2 Brand story — LOCKED
 
The name has two halves, and both point at the same thing.
 
**Val — from the Latin *valere*, to be worth, to be strong.** It is the root of *value*, *valid*, *valuable*, *valour*, *evaluate*, *prevail*. Everything Valstara sells is about worth: real goods at a fraction of list price, and the assurance that what you bought is what you get.
 
**Star — and it sits literally inside the name: VAL·STAR·A.** The star carries two meanings, and both are ours.
 
First, **the star is the native symbol of trust in commerce.** Every product on the internet carries a star rating. Every review is measured in stars. In a category where buyers arrive suspicious — where the default assumption is that they are about to be scammed — the star is the symbol they already look for.
 
Second, **the star is what you navigate by.** Before instruments, before maps that could be trusted, travellers steered by fixed points of light. A star is the thing that does not move when everything else does.
 
Put together: **Valstara is the mark you steer by to find real value.**
 
That is not decoration. It is the business. The category is full of anonymous marketplaces that connect buyers to sellers and disclaim everything in between. Valstara is the fixed point — the seller who owns the transaction, states the terms, and stands behind them.
 
### 1.3 Tagline — LOCKED
 
> **Premium for less.**
 
Short, unadorned, and true across every category we will ever sell. It states the commercial proposition. The trust proposition is carried by the Triple Guarantee (§2), not by the tagline — the two do different jobs and neither should try to do both.
 
Set in sentence case with a full stop. Never in all caps, never with an exclamation mark.
 
### 1.4 Positioning — LOCKED
 
> **Digital goods you know are real.**
 
We sell what others sell — subscriptions, software, keys, credit, items — well below list price. The difference is that we are the **seller**, not a listing board. When something goes wrong, it gets fixed, because there is no third party to point at.
 
**The gap we exploit:** the category leaders are marketplaces. That model scales, but it produces a structural trust deficit and a reputation that follows it. Buyers in this category are conditioned to expect a scam. Being the obvious exception is the entire strategy.
 
### 1.5 What we are / are not
 
| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| A direct seller — we own the transaction | A listing board or auction site |
| A guarantor — we resolve, replace, refund | A payment escrow |
| Category-broad over time | A gaming-only store |
| Priced well below list | The cheapest price on the internet |
| Precise, clean, contemporary | Edgy, hyped, or underground |
 
We do not win on price against marketplaces whose sellers undercut each other to zero margin. We win on **price-to-trust ratio.**
 
### 1.6 Brand pillars
 
1. **Worth.** Real products, well below list price.
2. **Certainty.** Stated terms, stated timeframes, honoured without argument.
3. **Precision.** No hype, no fake urgency, no dark patterns.
4. **Breadth.** One account for every kind of digital value.
### 1.7 Business model
 
Direct seller. Products are sourced from verified suppliers or acquired directly, then sold under the Valstara name at Valstara's own prices, with Valstara carrying full responsibility for fulfilment and support.
 
The long-term architecture is an **invisible marketplace**: multiple verified suppliers in the backend with automated order routing to whichever holds stock at the best price, while the customer sees one brand, one price, and one guarantee. Payout control remains with Valstara, which is what makes supplier accountability enforceable.
 
### 1.8 Markets
 
**Customers:** United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Germany, Spain, France, Italy, Romania, and all Nordic countries, with further European expansion planned.
 
**Suppliers:** sourced globally.

## 2. The Triple Guarantee — LOCKED
 
The most important asset in the brand. It is the productised form of the entire competitive position, and it is what the tagline deliberately does not attempt to carry.
 
### 2.1 The three promises
 
**I — Delivered within 24 hours.**
Every order is fulfilled by hand, Monday to Friday, within 24 hours of purchase.
 
**II — Protected, start to finish.**
256-bit encryption throughout. PCI-compliant payment handling. Your data is never sold.

*Amended 2026-10-01, founder-authorised. The promise previously read "never shared or sold". Email addresses necessarily reach the email providers so that mail can be delivered, and `docs/brand/copy-rules.md` forbids the "never share" claim for that reason.*
 
**III — Resolved, replaced, or refunded.**
If anything goes wrong at any point during your subscription period, we resolve it. If it cannot be resolved, we replace it. If it cannot be replaced, we refund you. In that order, without argument.
 
### 2.2 Why these three, and why in this order
 
They answer the three questions a suspicious buyer asks, in the order they ask them: *Will I actually get it? Is my money and data safe? What happens if it breaks?*
 
**On promise I.** We state the real number rather than claiming "instant." Instant delivery is what the category's worst actors claim and frequently fail to honour. A specific, honest, honoured timeframe outperforms a fast one that is doubted — and 24 hours, Monday to Friday, is a promise we can sustain at volume. **Never advertise instant delivery.**
 
**On promise III.** The escalation order is the promise. Most competitors offer either a replacement or a refund and make the customer fight for it. Stating the sequence up front removes the fight.
 
### 2.3 Price honesty
 
A fourth commitment sits on the guarantee page but is **not** one of the three: *the price you see is the price you pay.* No currency conversion tricks, no fees introduced at checkout, no mandatory service charges, no bait listings.
 
It belongs in the detail rather than the headline, because every competitor makes price claims and none of them can make the guarantee claims above.
 
### 2.4 Rules of use
 
- Always three. Never two, never four.
- Always numbered **I, II, III** — never 1, 2, 3, never bullets.
- Stated in full on a dedicated page; summarised on every product page.
- Warranty coverage stated per category in plain language, never "varies."
- **Never over-promise.** A broken guarantee is worse than no guarantee. Set terms at what can be honoured under supply stress, then honour them without exception.
### 2.5 The guarantee is a cost centre — fund it
 
Fragile supply produces correlated failures: a provider revocation sweep generates hundreds of simultaneous claims against a supply line that no longer exists. Hold a warranty reserve as a fixed percentage of revenue in high-risk categories. The guarantee is only a moat if it is funded.

## 3. Verbal identity
 
### 3.1 Voice
 
Plain, precise, unhurried. A company that has done this a long time and does not need to raise its voice.
 
- **Sentence case** everywhere. No title case, no all-caps emphasis.
- **Active voice.** "We replace it," not "replacement will be provided."
- **Specific over clever.** "Delivered within 24 hours" beats "lightning fast."
- **No exclamation marks** in product or transactional copy.
- **No manufactured urgency.** No countdown timers, no "3 people are viewing this," no invented scarcity. These are precisely the signals that make a suspicious buyer more suspicious.
### 3.2 Vocabulary
 
| Use | Never use |
|---|---|
| Sourced, verified supplier | Cracked, hacked, leaked |
| Regional pricing, promotional pricing | Black market, grey market, underground |
| Warranty, replacement, refund | No refunds, all sales final |
| Below list price | Dirt cheap, insane deal, unbeatable |
| Account, licence, key, credit | Shared account, stolen, bypass |
| Delivered within 24 hours | Instant delivery, immediate access |
| Digital goods | Digital products |
 
The left column is accurate **and** is what a legitimate company sounds like. The right column is what this category's worst actors sound like, and using it puts us in their bracket.
 
### 3.3 Naming conventions
 
- **Products** take the provider's name plainly: "Spotify Premium — 12 months." Never rename or obscure what someone is buying.
- **Programmes** take plain English names: the Triple Guarantee, the Supplier Register.
- **No sub-brands.** One brand across every category is the strategic point. Categories are navigation, not brands. Resist "Valstara Games," "Valstara AI."
### 3.4 Boilerplate
 
**One line:** Valstara sells digital goods with a guarantee.
 
**Short:** Valstara is a direct seller of digital goods — subscriptions, software licences, gift cards, game keys and in-game currency — at prices well below list. Every order is covered by the Triple Guarantee: delivered within 24 hours, protected start to finish, and resolved, replaced or refunded.
 
**Legal footer:** Valstara is a trading brand of 3NITY Digital Limited, registered in Hong Kong.

## 4. Visual identity
 
### 4.1 Design principle — LOCKED
 
**Clean, light-first, with coral as the single accent.**
 
The reference set is Apple, Stripe, Klarna and Booking — light, spacious, high-contrast, product-forward. Precision rather than decoration. Restraint rather than volume. Warm and contemporary rather than traditional.
 
Two rules follow:
 
1. **Light is a differentiator.** G2A, Eneba, Kinguin, IGGM and the skins marketplaces are almost uniformly dark, as are crypto and gambling. Light reads as legitimate, and the difference is visible in a single screenshot.
2. **Light is not sparse.** Light means legible and spacious while still being dense and colourful where products live. Product tiles carry each provider's own brand colour; the frame stays quiet so it can sit under all of them.
Dark mode ships as a **user toggle**, never the default.
 
### 4.2 The mark — LOCKED
 
A capital letter **V** constructed from strokes and three four-point stars.
 
**Construction:**
 
- Three four-point stars sit on a single horizontal line across the upper portion of the mark, all identical in size, evenly spaced.
- The **left** and **right** stars sit at the upper terminals of two outer strokes. Those strokes rise above the star line but are shortened, so they read as rays rather than as competing letterforms.
- The **centre** star floats within the V's opening, touching nothing.
- The V's two arms descend from the star line and meet at a sharp point at the vertex. **The V's arms dominate** — this was the decisive refinement, and the outer strokes must never be allowed to overpower them.
**Stroke specification:**
 
- **Even weight** along the entire length. No tapering, no thick-to-thin, no wedge or brush shapes.
- **Medium-heavy weight** — substantial and confident, but reading as a letterform, not a blunt chevron. Never thin or delicate.
- Perfectly straight. Approximately 45 degrees between the arms — narrow and upright, never a wide shallow arrow.
- **Perfect bilateral symmetry** about a vertical centre axis. Flipping the mark horizontally must produce an identical image. Asymmetric arms read as a checkmark and are wrong.
**Star geometry:**
 
- Sharp **four-point** stars with **concave curved sides**, curving inward to create needle-like points.
- **Vertical arms 1.4–1.6× longer than horizontal arms**, giving an upright elongated form.
- Sharp tips, no rounding. Solid filled, no outline.
- These must never soften into rounded sparkles. The soft four-point sparkle is the default AI-feature icon and is heavily overused.
**The V changes colour with its background:**
 
| Background | V colour | Stars |
|---|---|---|
| Light (`paper`, `surface`) | Near-black `#101014` | Coral |
| Dark (`midnight`, near-black) | Near-white `#FAFAFA` | Coral |
 
The stars are always coral. Only the V inverts.
 
### 4.3 Flat and gradient — LOCKED
 
Two treatments, each with a defined job.
 
**Flat coral `#E8654A`** — the master. Used on light backgrounds, in the website header, in print, in embroidery, and anywhere a single spot colour is required.
 
**Gradient coral `#E8654A → #FF8A66`** — used inside the app-icon container and in dark-surface applications only.
 
**Gradient specification:** a subtle linear ramp running lower-left to upper-right, consistent across all three stars as if lit from a single source. The two ends must be close enough in value that each star reads as **one hue with depth**, never as two colours. No metallic sheen, no foil, no chrome, no specular highlights, no banding.
 
### 4.4 The app-icon container — LOCKED
 
The mark is designed **container-first** for icon use.
 
- A **rounded square** with a corner radius of roughly **22% of the width** — the modern squircle proportion, never a small 8px radius.
- Solid **midnight `#171733`** fill. An optional subtle gradient to a deeper `#0D0D1F` is permitted.
- The mark centred, occupying roughly **55–60% of the container width**, with generous optical padding on all sides. Never crowding the edges.
- V in near-white `#FAFAFA`, stars in gradient coral.
Used for: app icon, favicon, social avatars, Discord server icon, merchandise.
 
### 4.5 The wordmark — LOCKED
 
**Valstara** — sentence case, capital V only, set in the primary typeface (§6) at a medium or semibold weight. Normal tracking, tight fit. Near-black on light, near-white on dark. Never coloured.
 
### 4.6 Lockups
 
1. **Horizontal** — mark left, wordmark right, optically balanced. The default.
2. **Container + wordmark** — squircle icon left, wordmark right. For app stores and profile headers.
3. **Mark alone** — favicon, avatar, merch, jersey.
4. **Stacked** — mark above wordmark, centred. For square placements.
**Clear space:** minimum of the wordmark's cap height on all sides.
 
### 4.7 Colourways
 
| Version | Use |
|---|---|
| **Flat coral stars, near-black V** | Primary, on light backgrounds |
| **Gradient coral stars, near-white V, midnight container** | App icon, avatars, merch — the strongest version |
| **Flat coral stars, near-white V** | Dark surfaces without a container |
| **All near-black** | Single-colour print, embossing, engraving |
| **All near-white** | Single-colour on dark |
 
### 4.8 Small sizes
 
The full mark does not survive below roughly 24px. A **simplified variant** is required for favicon and avatar use: outer stars removed, centre star retained, strokes rising to full height as a clean symmetrical V, at a heavier stroke weight with a generous gap between the strokes and the star.
 
This variant is for small sizes only and never replaces the full mark.
 
### 4.9 Misuse
 
Never: recolour outside the palette · stretch or skew · taper the strokes · add drop shadows, glows, bevels or 3D · rotate · outline the stars · substitute five-, six- or eight-pointed stars · alter the number of stars · break symmetry · colour the wordmark · apply a gradient strong enough to read as two colours · apply metallic or foil effects · set the wordmark in all caps in body or interface contexts.
 
### 4.10 Rejected directions
 
Explored and discarded. Do not reintroduce:
 
- **Gold** — carries the star-rating convention, but reads traditional and luxury-coded. It is saturated in premium, heritage and luxury branding and near-absent from digital-goods commerce for good reason. Wrong register for an audience buying AI tools and game keys.
- **Five-pointed stars** — collides with Rockstar Games, whose products we sell; also the most generic star form in existence.
- **Green** — collides with Trustpilot, whose widgets appear on our own product pages, and is the default for review platforms.
- **Red** — G2A already owns red-orange in this category, and it collides with Netflix, YouTube and Adobe product tiles. Note that coral at `#E8654A` is materially lower in saturation and separates cleanly from all of them; this was tested.
- **Purple** — reads as Twitch, and Eneba already occupies it.
- **Three stars in a level row, floating loose** — reads as a three-out-of-five rating.
- **Soft rounded AI sparkles** — overused default for AI features.
- **Multi-hue and gradient-mesh marks** — contemporary but not ownable; the three-coloured-blob app icon is a 2022–2024 signature that is already dating.

| `coral-lift` | `#FF8A66` | Gradient terminal; small marks needing brightness |

| `midnight` | `#171733` | Dark surfaces — icon container, guarantee band, footer |
| `midnight-deep` | `#0D0D1F` | Optional gradient terminal within the container |
 
**Dark mode (toggle):** `paper` `#0F0F1A` · `surface` `#171733` · `ink` `#F0F0EE` · `coral` `#FF8A66` · `ink-600` `#A0A0A8`

`ink-600`'s dark value (`#A0A0A8`, the `ink-300` grey) is an accessibility override authorised 2026-09-05: secondary text at the base `#55555E` fails WCAG AA on the dark grounds, and reusing the existing `ink-300` grey clears AA while keeping the muted hierarchy identical to light.
 
**Functional, UI only:** `error` `#C0392B` · `warning` `#B7791F` · `success` `#2E7D57`. These never appear in brand materials. Note that `error` must be visibly distinct from `coral` — never use coral for error states.
 
### 5.1 The coral rule
 
**Coral does not carry body text.** At `#E8654A` on `paper` it clears WCAG AA for large text but fails for body copy. Use it for the mark, buttons, links, large headings above 24px, badges and accents. Verify contrast per use.
 
Coral must read **warm and contemporary, never neon and never brick.** If it drifts bright it reads as a discount sticker; if it drifts dark it reads as rust. `#E8654A` is the anchor.
 
### 5.2 Midnight as contrast
 
Light is the default, but a page that is uniformly light reads flat. **Midnight bands** provide the page's contrast structure: the guarantee band, the footer, and selected hero panels. This is deliberate and is what prevents the editorial flatness a purely light page produces.
 
### 5.3 Provider brand colours
 
**Product tiles use each provider's own brand colour freely** — Netflix red, Spotify green, Adobe red, Prime Video blue. These are the visual system of the storefront and what customers scan for. The brand palette governs the page frame; provider colours govern the products.
 
This is precisely why the frame is neutral. A coloured frame would fight every product on the page.
 
Coral was tested directly against Netflix red `#E50914`, YouTube red `#FF0000`, Crunchyroll orange `#F47521` and Spotify green `#1DB954`, and separates cleanly from all of them on both light and dark backgrounds.
 
### 5.4 Accessibility
 
Body text must clear WCAG AA (4.5:1). `ink` on `paper` and `paper` on `midnight` both pass comfortably. `coral` on `paper` passes for large text only — see §5.1.

## 6. Typography — LOCKED
 
| Role | Face | Fallback | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Söhne** (Klim Type Foundry) | **Instrument Sans** (open source) | Everything — navigation, headings, body, buttons, labels, wordmark |
| **Data** | **Söhne Mono** | **JetBrains Mono** | Prices, order numbers, licence keys, codes |
 
**Why Söhne.** Apple's SF Pro is not licensable for general web use. Söhne is the closest equivalent with a proper licence path — a neo-grotesque with the precision the brand needs and a warmth that Inter lacks. It is what Stripe-tier products use, and it will not read as a template.
 
**Explicitly ruled out:** Inter, Poppins, Montserrat, Playfair Display. All competent, all the default answer, all instantly recognisable as such.
 
**No serif type anywhere in the interface.**
 
### 6.1 Scale
 
H1 32/40 · H2 24/32 · H3 20/28 · Body 16/24 · Small 14/20 · Label 13/18
 
Tight headline tracking, clear weight hierarchy. Section headings **left-aligned, sentence case** — never centred, never small caps, never letterspaced.
 
### 6.2 Price display
 
- Prices large and bold, in monospace with **tabular figures**
- List price struck through in `ink-600`; our price in `ink`
- Savings shown as a **pill badge** in `coral`, calculated from actual values
- Currency symbol at numeral size, never superscript

## 7. UI system — DIRECTIONAL
 
**Layout.** Full-width content on a strict grid. Wide container, narrow side margins. Product rows of 4–5 cards across, equal width, grid-aligned. Sections sit directly on the page background — no boxed bands, no narrow centred column, no editorial margins.
 
**Depth.** Cards sit on soft realistic drop shadows, not hairline outlines. Layered elevation: hero above background, cards above section, badges above cards.
 
**Radii.** 12px cards · 8px buttons and inputs · 999px pills.
 
**Spacing.** 24px minimum card padding. 64–80px between sections. Nothing cramped.
 
**Imagery.** Every product tile is a **composed promotional banner** — device mockup or app UI, provider logo, a discount figure as a design element, layered background. **A logo centred on a flat colour block is not acceptable.** That is the clearest tell of an unfinished storefront.
 
**Density.** Products appear **high** on the page, immediately after the hero. Buyers arrive with intent and need to see products and prices without scrolling.
 
**Micro-detail.** Visible hover states. Coral discount pills. Star glyphs. Cart count badge. Subtle dividers, never heavy rules.
 
**Motion.** 150–200ms ease-out on state changes. No bounce, no parallax, no auto-carousels beyond the hero. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
 
### 7.1 Homepage structure
 
1. **Header** — mark and wordmark left · wide rounded search with a dark action button · currency and language · account · help · cart with count
2. **Nav** — Categories · AI · Subscriptions · Software · Gift cards · Game keys · Game top-ups · In-game currency
3. **Hero** — a full-width banner block showing actual products and prices. Never a centred headline with empty space around it
4. **Trust bar** — Trustpilot rating · Delivered within 24 hours · Verified suppliers · Full order protection
5. **Category rail** — horizontally scrolling provider tiles
6. **AI subscriptions** — left-aligned heading, product card row
7. **Deals row** — wide composed promotional banners
8. **Streaming and entertainment** — product card row
9. **Guarantee band** — full-width `midnight`, three columns, Roman numerals in `coral`: I Delivered within 24 hours · II Protected, start to finish · III Resolved, replaced, or refunded. This band provides the page's principal contrast
10. **Reviews** — Trustpilot-style cards
11. **Footer** — `midnight`, four link columns, mark, payment logos, legal line
### 7.2 Product page
 
Required, in order: product name in plain language → what you actually receive → delivery timeframe → price with list price struck through → **Triple Guarantee summary** → Trustpilot rating → supplier verification note.
 
The guarantee sits **above the fold, next to the price.** Not a footer element.
 
### 7.3 Anti-patterns
 
Cream or warm backgrounds · centred section headings · centred hero text · narrow content columns with wide empty margins · editorial, magazine or newspaper layouts · small caps or letterspaced labels · hairline rules as section dividers · serif type in the interface · text-only product cards · logos on flat colour blocks in place of artwork · cramped padding · wireframe or template aesthetics · countdown timers · fake stock scarcity · starbursts · exit-intent popups · claims of instant delivery.

## 8. Other applications
 
### 8.1 Clip and social overlays
 
- Brand bug in a bottom corner: mark + `valstara.com` on one line. Nothing else.
- Test the clean bug against a sales-text overlay — promotional overlays are frequently suppressed in reach, and a large distribution penalty for a small conversion gain is a bad trade.
- Never obscure the action in the clip.
- Point traffic to something we own — Discord or email — rather than straight to checkout.
- Run several accounts from the outset. These platforms are rented land.
### 8.2 Discord and community
 
Community is where a guarantee-led brand earns loyalty, and it doubles as the review-generation engine.
 
- Container mark as server icon; full lockup for pinned and official posts.
- Named support presence with stated response times.
- No hype posting, no spam drops, no @everyone for promotions.
### 8.3 Reviews
 
Review volume is a **lagging** indicator of scale, not a cause of it. We cannot out-review incumbents with hundreds of thousands of reviews. We can cross the credibility threshold — a few hundred to a few thousand genuine reviews at 4.5★+ — and then compete on the guarantee.
 
- Request on **successful delivery**, not on order.
- Ramp velocity gradually. Spikes are flagged as inauthentic.
- Never buy reviews. It is the fastest possible way to destroy a trust brand.
### 8.4 Merch and esports
 
Container mark or flat mark, coral on midnight or near-black fabric. The mark scales to embroidery without modification, which was a requirement of its selection. Use the flat treatment for embroidery — gradients do not thread.
 
**Note:** merchandise and apparel carry a specific trademark risk — see §12.

## 9. Category expansion — LOCKED
 
The brand is category-neutral by design. Neither half of the name binds it to a product type.
 
Categories are **navigation, not sub-brands.** Each launches under the same mark, the same guarantee, and the same voice:
 
1. Subscriptions — entertainment and **AI** *(current wedge)*
2. Software licences
3. Gift cards and mobile game top-ups
4. Game keys
5. In-game currency and items
6. eSIMs
**The rule:** no category launches without a stated delivery timeframe and warranty we can honour. The guarantee defines the catalogue, not the other way round.

## 10. Partnership guardrails — LOCKED
 
The long-term plan includes creator sponsorship, an owned esports team, owned tournaments, and eventually owned media. The brand is built to carry that. These rules protect it on the way.
 
### 10.1 Never associate with
 
**Gambling, casino, skin-gambling, case-opening.** This is the dominant sponsorship playbook in CS2 and it is under active pressure — Valve has banned skin-gambling and case-opening sponsors from CS2 jerseys and broadcasts, and Twitch restricts gambling streams. Adopting it would trade the entire trust position for short-term reach.
 
**Nicotine and alcohol.** Two candidate names were rejected during naming for precisely this reason — one collided with a major nicotine pouch brand, another with a beer brand. Age-restricted adjacency is incompatible with a brand intending to sponsor creators across categories.
 
**Adult content.**
 
### 10.2 Sequencing
 
Micro-creator affiliate and revenue-share deals are the correct early form. Owning a team or a tournament only makes sense once marketing budgets run to seven figures. This is a later milestone gated on revenue, not an early brand investment.
 
### 10.3 Creator fit
 
We sponsor creators whose audiences buy digital goods, in **any** content category — not gaming exclusively. The name was chosen so that a non-gaming creator can say it without it sounding out of place.
